How the Calculator Works

1. MAP (Maximal Aerobic Power) interpolation

MAP is the highest power you reached, adjusted for the unfinished stage:

MAP = last completed watts + (unfinished seconds ÷ step duration) × step size

Unfinished time is capped at the protocol’s step length so a partial stage never exceeds a full step.

Examples

  • Last completed 250 W, 30 s unfinished, male protocol (25 W / 60 s) → MAP = 250 + (30/60) × 25 = 262.5 W (displayed as 263 W).
  • GP Lama: 375 W completed + 120 s of a 150 s stage → MAP = 375 + (120/150) × 25 = 395 W.

2. FTP from MAP

FTP = MAP × factor

Advanced settings let you override step size, step duration, FTP percentage, and workout start power.

3. VO2 max (Hawley–Noakes 1992)

VO2max (L/min) = 0.01141 × MAP + 0.435

VO2max (ml/kg/min) = VO2max (L/min) × 1000 ÷ body weight (kg)

Weight is optional. Without it, W/kg and relative VO2 max stay blank. Pair this estimate with the VO2 Max Calculator or the how to calculate VO2 max guide.

4. 20-minute target & Coggan zones

20-min target = FTP ÷ 0.95 (the classic conversion from a 20-minute test).

Zones are standard Coggan percentages of FTP. Compare W/kg with the Power-to-Weight Calculator.

Which Protocol Should You Use?

  • Non-elite male (25 W / 60 s, 75%) — Most common starting point for male riders of average fitness.
  • Female (15 W / 60 s, 75%) — Smaller steps improve resolution and reduce the chance of overshooting.
  • Elite male (20 W / 60 s, 75%) — Slightly finer steps for higher-level athletes.
  • GP Lama (25 W / 150 s, 82.5%) — Longer stages and higher conversion factor popularized by Shane Miller. Can produce higher FTP numbers; validate with training or a 20-minute test if the result feels optimistic.

Ramp tests are highly reproducible and require no pacing skill, but they can overestimate FTP for anaerobic-dominant riders and slightly underestimate for strong diesel engines. Many athletes confirm the number with a 20-minute effort or simply monitor how the resulting zones feel in subsequent workouts.

FAQ

What is the difference between MAP and FTP?

MAP is peak aerobic power from the ramp. FTP is the highest power you can roughly sustain for about an hour and is estimated as a percentage of MAP (usually 75%, or 82.5% for the GP Lama protocol).

Why interpolate the unfinished stage?

Stopping part-way through a step means you produced more work than the previous completed stage. Linear interpolation gives a fairer MAP than simply taking the last finished step.

Is the VO2 max estimate accurate?

It uses the published Hawley–Noakes (1992) relationship between peak power and VO2 max and is a solid field estimate for trained cyclists. It is not a substitute for laboratory gas-exchange testing.

Should I validate the FTP?

Yes, especially the first time you use a new protocol. Aim for roughly FTP / 0.95 as a 20-minute average, or simply watch how the zones feel over the next 1–2 weeks and adjust if needed.

How do I use the .zwo file?

Download the file, place it in your Zwift workouts folder, set in-game FTP to 100 W, then start the workout in ERG mode.

Does weight affect FTP?

No. Weight is used only for W/kg and relative VO2 max.
