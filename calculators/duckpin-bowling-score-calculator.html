What Is Duckpin Bowling?

Duckpin bowling is a variation of traditional 10-pin bowling, most popular on the East Coast of the United States. Key differences include:

  • Smaller, lighter pins
  • Smaller balls without finger holes
  • Three throws allowed in every frame
  • Strikes are significantly harder to achieve

Because of the difficulty, players get three attempts per frame instead of two.

How Duckpin Scoring Works

Scoring is similar to regular 10-pin bowling, with one important difference:

  • Strike (X) = 10 points + the next two rolls
  • Spare (/) = 10 points + the next one roll
  • Open frame = sum of the three throws in that frame (maximum 10 points)

If you clear all 10 pins using all three balls, that frame simply scores 10 points (it does not carry a bonus into the next frame like a spare does).

Note: This calculator can also be used for candlepin bowling, which follows the same three-ball scoring rules.

Quick Tips for Better Duckpin Scores

  • Focus on consistent spare conversion — strikes are rare
  • Use all three balls strategically
  • Keep your approach and release consistent
  • Track scores over time to measure improvement
